Task Details: Evaluating LLM-Powered AI Tutors

Motivation

With the rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs), AI-powered tutoring systems are emerging as promising tools for delivering scalable and personalized education. Despite this progress, their pedagogical effectiveness—particularly in multilingual and diverse educational settings—remains an open research challenge. This shared task aims to systematically evaluate the instructional capabilities of LLMs, focusing on their ability to identify learner mistakes and provide effective guidance.

Objective

This task focuses on evaluating educational dialogues between an AI tutor and a student, specifically in the context of mathematics learning. The conversations are centered around student mistakes or points of confusion, with the tutor expected to respond in a way that effectively remediates these issues. Participants will develop models that analyze the dialogue history and assess whether the AI tutor's latest response appropriately addresses the student's error. The evaluation emphasizes pedagogical quality, such as correctness, relevance, clarity, and instructional value of the tutor’s response.

Track Details

The shared task consists of two tracks. In each track, participants will evaluate AI tutor responses using specific labels, defined below:

Track 1: Mistake Identification

Determine whether the AI tutor has correctly identified a mistake in the student’s input.

Yes: The tutor correctly identifies and explicitly addresses the student's mistake.
No: The tutor fails to identify or completely misses the mistake in the student’s input.
To some extent: The tutor partially identifies the issue, but the response is incomplete or imprecise.

Track 2: Providing Guidance

Assess whether the AI tutor has provided meaningful and pedagogically sound guidance in response to the student’s error.

Yes: The tutor offers clear, helpful, and contextually appropriate guidance that facilitates learning.
No: The guidance is missing, misleading, or irrelevant to the student's input.
To some extent: The tutor provides some guidance, but it lacks clarity, depth, or completeness.

Participants are encouraged to take into account linguistic variation, pedagogical quality, and contextual relevance while developing their systems. The task aims to foster robust evaluation and promote the design of trustworthy and effective AI tutors across languages and learning settings.

Dataset Description

We constructed the train, dev-test, and test splits from the MRBench dataset, which was used in the BEA Shared Task 2025 (Kochmar et al., 2025). The dataset builds on mathematical dialogues from the MathDial (Macina et al., 2023) and Bridge (Wang et al., 2024) datasets, representing secondary and primary school-level mathematics, respectively. It comprises conversation histories between students and tutors, where the last few student utterances often contain confusion or mistakes. Tutor responses are generated by seven LLMs acting as AI tutors (GPT-4, Sonnet, Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.1-405B, Gemini, Mistral, Phi-3) and two human tutors (Expert and Novice), aiming to mitigate student confusion or mistakes. Novice tutor responses are available only for the Bridge dialogues. Each response is annotated with one of three labels — Yes, No, or To some extent — to assess its pedagogical appropriateness for Mistake Identification and Providing Guidance. For more details on the annotation guidelines and the initial release of MRBench, please refer to Maurya et al., 2025.

Key Descriptions

  • conversation_id: A unique identifier for each dialogue instance.
  • conversation_history: The conversation history between tutor and student.
  • response: The reply generated by either an LLM or human tutor intended to address the student's misunderstanding.
  • annotation: Labels that indicate whether the tutor's response correctly identifies the student's mistake or whether it provides pedagogical guidance. Only present for training data.

All datasets will be available for download closer to the start date of the competition.

Evaluation

All submissions will be evaluated using both Macro F1-Score and Accuracy. A public leaderboard will be hosted on the CodaBench platform, displaying scores for Macro F1 and Accuracy. Final rankings will be determined based on the Macro F1-Score as the primary evaluation metric.
